## Full permutation proof

After gap6, stream r consists of post-pass slots r,r+6,r+12,..., each nonincreasing by unsigned u32 priority and stable on its own ties. Let j be a current post-gap6 slot. The pair `(priority,−j)` uniquely ranks all elements. Equivalently, `((u64(priority)<<9) | (511−j))` occupies at most41bits and is injective for key u32 and j0..511. This is a proof representation, not a required runtime packing choice. Key0/slot511 has rank0, so exhaustion must not silently use rank0 as a sentinel; track active streams explicitly or prove a different sentinel.

A stream head is at least every later element of its stream under this rank: higher priority wins, or equal priority has smaller post-pass slot. Therefore the best active head is the best remaining element globally. Emit it, advance that stream by6, repeat. Induction gives exactly the stable decreasing order of the post-gap6 sequence, which is the released second stage (`tactical-capture-order.md:9–26`). Opaque u16 Moves are carried unchanged; duplicates are distinguished by slot, never by move equality or decoded tags. Full unsigned keys, including0/1 and u32::MAX, are valid for this internal permutation proof, even though native preferred construction selects>1.

Original-input stable ties are **wrong**: `[2,5,2,2,2,2,5]` moves item6 before item1 during gap6; the final5 tie must retain that post-pass order. All-equal, empty, singleton, uneven stream lengths and n512 follow the same proof. No legal-game or material assumption is used.

## Callback, storage and domain closure

The existing loop calls the priority callback exactly once per original candidate in original order before any sorting/output (`347–356`). Keep it unchanged, including preferred>1 reversal and deferred0/1 stability. For a valid native MoveList m<=512, low+n=m gives low<=first, so workspace low[0..low] and preferred[first..512] cannot overlap; equality means they abut. Each head starts r<n and is dereferenced only while its cursor<n; advancing at most to n+5<=517 fits either usize or u16. Output writes exactly n slots, then low slots ending at m<=512. The original MoveList and workspace are distinct safe mutable arguments, so writing final output cannot overwrite unread stream/deferred inputs.

A callback panic occurs before merge and leaves the original list at the same stage as today. No new fallible callback or allocation is introduced by the model. Workspace priority cells need not end globally sorted: fields are private and every later preferred cell is overwritten before reading. The observable API output is the MoveList; do not make that claim about the separate public priority_sequence API, which must still permute both supplied slices exactly as before. Source-owned valid lengths eliminate internal indexing/overflow failure only if the implementation actually follows these bounds; tests/backend still required.
