**Exact arithmetic, including extremes.** Let B=2^32, k in[0,B−1], and 1<=d<=B−1. Store m=floor(B/d) as u64 (not u32: d=1 gives m=B). Set q=floor(k*m/B), r=k−q*d. Because m<=B/d and 0<=B/d−m<1, 0<=k/d−k*m/B<k/B<1. Therefore q is either floor(k/d) or one less. Hence r is the true remainder or true remainder+d, and one `if r>=d { r-=d }` is exact.

All operations fit ordinary u64 arithmetic: k*m<=(B−1)*B=2^64−2^32; q*d<=k<B, so subtraction cannot underflow; provisional r<=k<B, even though the looser mathematical bound r<2d can exceed B. For d=1, m=B, q=k and r=0 even at k=u32MAX. For d=u32MAX, m=1 and k=d is exactly the case corrected from r=d to0. For d>u32MAX (including d=B), k<d, so return k without narrowing d or constructing a small-divisor reciprocal. This covers the x86-64 usize domain; d=0 remains excluded by existing constructors. Form B with a u64 shift and widen before multiplication/division. No saturation, wrapping trick or floating-point approximation is needed. The large-count arithmetic domain can be proven/tested without attempting an impossible huge Group allocation.
